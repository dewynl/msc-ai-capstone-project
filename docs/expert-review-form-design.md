# Expert Review Form Design: AI-Generated Educational Syllabi

## Purpose

Evaluate the pedagogical quality, coherence, and usability of AI-generated course syllabi from the educational function-calling system.

**Target Participants:** 5-10 educational experts (instructors, curriculum designers, educational technologists)

**Estimated Time:** 20-30 minutes

### Section 2: Review Instructions

**Display Text:**

> **About This Study**
>
> You will review 3-5 course syllabi generated by an AI system designed to create structured, pedagogically sound educational content. The system uses:
> - A fine-tuned CodeT5 language model
> - Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for educational components
> - A function-calling architecture to ensure structural validity
>
> **What to Evaluate:**
> - **Pedagogical Quality:** Is the content educationally appropriate?
> - **Coherence:** Do the modules, activities, and assessments fit together logically?
> - **Completeness:** Does the syllabus include all necessary components?
> - **Usability:** Would you use this syllabus (with or without modifications)?
>
> **Note:** These syllabi are AI-generated. Please evaluate them as you would any draft syllabus from a colleague or student.

## Implementation Notes

### Syllabi to Include

**Recommendation:** Include 3-5 syllabi with diversity:

1. **Beginner Computer Science** (e.g., Intro to Python)
   - Tests basic content generation
   - Accessible to most reviewers

2. **Intermediate Cross-Domain** (e.g., Data Science, Business Analytics)
   - Tests handling of interdisciplinary content
   - Different activity types

3. **Advanced Technical** (e.g., Machine Learning, Advanced Algorithms)
   - Tests depth of technical content
   - Complex assessment structures

4. **Non-CS Domain** (e.g., Digital Marketing, Statistics)
   - Tests generalization beyond computer science
   - Different pedagogical approaches

5. **Edge Case** (e.g., Short course, workshop format)
   - Tests flexibility of system
   - Different duration/format

### Google Form Settings

**Settings to Enable:**
- [ ] Collect email addresses (for follow-up, optional)
- [ ] Limit to 1 response per person
- [ ] Allow editing after submit (in case they want to revise)
- [ ] Show progress bar
- [ ] Shuffle question order (for some sections to reduce order bias)

**Response Options:**
- [ ] Send respondent a copy of their responses
- [ ] Allow summary charts and text responses viewing (disable for privacy)

### Pilot Testing

**Before distributing widely:**

1. **Internal pilot** (1-2 friendly colleagues)
   - Check clarity of questions
   - Estimate completion time
   - Identify confusing wording

2. **Small external pilot** (2-3 experts)
   - Validate that questions capture useful feedback
   - Ensure rating scales are used as intended
   - Check for missing dimensions

### Recruitment Strategy

**Target:** 8-12 completed responses (recruit 15-20 to account for non-response)

**Recruitment Channels:**
1. **Direct outreach** to faculty in your network
2. **Department email lists** (with permission)
3. **LinkedIn** - education technology groups
4. **Academic Twitter** - education researcher community
5. **Professional associations** (e.g., ACM SIGCSE, Learning Sciences)

**Incentives to Consider:**
- Co-authorship on paper if contribution is substantial
- Amazon gift card ($25-50) for completed review
- Access to final system/tool once complete
- Recognition in dissertation acknowledgments

### Data Analysis Plan

**Quantitative Analysis:**
- Calculate mean, median, std dev for all Likert scale questions
- Inter-rater agreement (if multiple reviewers see same syllabus)
- Correlation between reviewer expertise and ratings
- Pass threshold: Mean score ≥ 3.5 on key dimensions

**Qualitative Analysis:**
- Thematic analysis of open-ended responses
- Categorize strengths and weaknesses mentioned
- Identify common concerns and improvement suggestions
- Quote representative feedback in dissertation

**Dissertation Use:**
- **Chapter 5 (Results):** Present quantitative scores, comparison across syllabi
- **Chapter 6 (Discussion):** Interpret findings, discuss limitations, expert feedback themes
- **Appendix:** Full raw data (anonymized), sample syllabi shown to experts

## Sample Size Justification

**For dissertation purposes:**

| Sample Size | Statistical Power | Qualitative Richness | Feasibility |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------------|-------------|
| 3-5 experts | Low | Minimal | Very Easy |
| 6-8 experts | Moderate | Adequate | Easy |
| **8-12 experts** | **Good** | **Rich** | **Moderate** |
| 15-20 experts | High | Very Rich | Difficult |

**Recommendation: Target 8-12 completed responses**

**Justification:**
- Small-scale qualitative study typical for MSc
- Sufficient for thematic saturation
- Feasible within dissertation timeline
- Acceptable for educational research (not clinical trial)

**Citation:**
> "For qualitative educational research, 6-12 expert participants is typically sufficient for reaching thematic saturation and providing credible validation." (Creswell & Poth, 2018)
